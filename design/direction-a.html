LAB-014 · Vector retrieval · Measured 2026-10-14 · Revised 2026-10-21

HNSW recall@10 falls to 0.71 after 40% churn, and only a full rebuild brings it back

TL;DR

Deleting and re-inserting vectors into an HNSW index degrades recall far faster than inserting new ones. At 40% cumulative churn on a 1M-vector index, recall@10 drops from 0.943 to 0.713. Periodic repacking holds the line at 0.861 but costs 18% of steady-state CPU. A full rebuild restores 0.941 and takes 51 minutes on this box.

If your corpus churns, budget for rebuilds. Treat the index as a cache with a TTL, not as a database.

01The question

Every vector-database benchmark I could find measures a static index: build it once, query it, report recall against queries-per-second. Production corpora are not static. Documents are edited, re-embedded and written back, which in HNSW means a delete-plus-insert — and a delete in HNSW is usually a tombstone, not a removal.

So: how much does recall@10 degrade as a function of cumulative churn, and which of the three things you can actually do about it is worth doing?

02Setup

One machine, nothing else running on it, every version pinned. The corpus is 1,000,000 passages from MS MARCO v2 embedded with bge-small-en-v1.5 (384-d, cosine). Churn is simulated by sampling without replacement, re-embedding with 2% token-level noise, deleting and re-inserting.

03Result

The no-maintenance curve does not decay smoothly. It is flat to roughly 20% churn, falls off a cliff between 30% and 40%, then flattens again at a lower level — the graph has lost connectivity it cannot repair by itself.

Limitations

  1. One embedding model, one corpus. bge-small-en-v1.5 at 384-d on MS MARCO may be unusually sensitive to graph damage; a 1024-d model with more redundant neighbourhoods could degrade differently, and I have not run it.
  2. Churn here is synthetic and uniformly sampled. Real corpora churn in hot spots — a few documents edited constantly — which almost certainly concentrates the damage and makes these numbers optimistic.
  3. 7.6 GB of RAM forces M=16. A larger M builds a more redundant graph that probably tolerates more churn, and I could not test above M=24 without swapping, which would have invalidated the latency column.

04Reproduce

Clone the repo and run one command. It pulls the pinned corpus shard, builds the index, runs all six arms and writes results.csv. Budget about 6 hours on a comparable box.

docker run --rm -v "$PWD/out:/out" ghcr.io/ankitmishra/lab:014 \
  --arms all --seeds 12 --corpus msmarco-v2-1m --out /out
