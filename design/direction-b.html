LAB-014Vector retrieval Measured 2026-10-14 · rev 2026-10-21

HNSW recall@10 falls to 0.71 after 40% churn, and only a full rebuild brings it back

TL;DR

Deleting and re-inserting vectors degrades an HNSW index far faster than inserting new ones. At 40% cumulative churn on 1M vectors, recall@10 goes 0.943 → 0.713. Periodic repacking holds 0.861 for 18% of steady-state CPU. A full rebuild restores 0.941 in 51 minutes on this box.

If your corpus churns, budget for rebuilds. Treat the index as a cache with a TTL, not as a database.

§01The question

Every vector-database benchmark I could find measures a static index: build once, query, report recall against queries-per-second. Production corpora are not static. Documents are edited, re-embedded and written back, which in HNSW is a delete plus an insert — and a delete in HNSW is usually a tombstone, not a removal.

So: how far does recall@10 fall as a function of cumulative churn, and which of the three things you can actually do about it is worth doing?

§02Setup

One machine, nothing else running on it, every version pinned. 1,000,000 MS MARCO v2 passages embedded with bge-small-en-v1.5 (384-d, cosine). Churn is simulated by sampling without replacement, re-embedding with 2% token-level noise, deleting, re-inserting.

§03Result

The no-maintenance curve does not decay smoothly. It is flat to about 20% churn, falls off a cliff between 30% and 40%, then flattens at a lower level — the graph has lost connectivity it cannot repair by itself.

Limitations
  1. One embedding model, one corpus. bge-small-en-v1.5 at 384-d may be unusually sensitive to graph damage; a 1024-d model with more redundant neighbourhoods could degrade differently, and I have not run it.
  2. Churn is synthetic and uniformly sampled. Real corpora churn in hot spots — a few documents edited constantly — which almost certainly concentrates the damage and makes these numbers optimistic.
  3. 7.6 GB of RAM forces M=16. A larger M builds a more redundant graph that probably tolerates more churn; above M=24 this box swaps, which would have invalidated the latency column.

§04Reproduce

One command. It pulls the pinned corpus shard, builds the index, runs all six arms and writes results.csv. Budget about 6 hours on a comparable box.

docker run --rm -v "$PWD/out:/out" ghcr.io/ankitmishra/lab:014 \
  --arms all --seeds 12 --corpus msmarco-v2-1m --out /out
